How the comparison works

  • Both paths start with the same money. The buyer spends the down payment and closing costs; the renter invests that cash instead (minus any move-in costs).
  • Each month, whichever path costs less invests the difference at your investment return. Buying costs include mortgage, property tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, and PMI, minus any tax savings. Renting costs are rent and renters insurance, rising each year.
  • At the end, the buyer sells: home value minus selling costs minus the remaining loan, plus any investments. The renter keeps their investments. The higher net worth wins.
  • Break-even rent is the starting rent at which both paths finish even over your time frame. If you can rent for less than that, renting comes out ahead.
  • Simplifications: no capital gains tax on the home (most sales fall under the primary-residence exclusion), and the investment return should already be after tax. Commute time only enters the result if you tick the box above.
